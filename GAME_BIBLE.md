# GAME BIBLE — Through the Jade Wall

## High concept

A young traveler known only as **Alice** follows a white mahjong tile through a rain-soaked alley and falls through a tea table into **Jade Court**, a city whose streets, memories, inhabitants, and physical laws are arranged like a never-ending Mahjong hand.

Alice is not trying to “win Mahjong.” She is trying to understand why the city insists that every life must fit into a valid hand.

The deeper truth: Jade Court is a memory engine built to reconstruct one disputed night. Every reset rearranges the evidence. Alice appears to be an outsider, but she may be the missing tile that makes the system complete.

## Core fantasy

The player should feel:

- curious rather than powerful;
- clever when they notice a rule;
- uncertain when the same evidence supports incompatible stories;
- increasingly capable of manipulating the city's grammar;
- personally implicated in the final interpretation.

## Design pillars

### 1. Mahjong is physics

Tiles define relationships in the world:

- sequences align spaces;
- triplets stabilize repeated objects;
- pairs bind identities;
- winds rotate districts and authority;
- dragons alter exceptional rules;
- discards remove possibilities;
- calls expose hidden relationships.

### 2. Wonderland logic

Rules are internally consistent but initially absurd.
Examples:

- a corridor grows longer when Alice runs;
- a door becomes smaller when approached;
- a staircase only connects floors after three numbered lanterns form a sequence;
- an NPC can be in two locations until the player forms a pair.

### 3. Truth is assembled

The player constructs interpretations from fragments.
The game never reduces morality to a good/evil meter.

### 4. Small world, high density

Every room should contribute at least two of:

- navigation;
- puzzle;
- narrative;
- mechanical teaching;
- visual foreshadowing;
- player choice.

### 5. Respect the player's intelligence

No quest marker should replace observation.
Hints should clarify the rules, not solve the puzzle.

## Player loop

Explore → observe pattern → test a rule → manipulate tile/world state → reveal path or memory → update interpretation → choose what to preserve/discard.

## Main systems

- third-person movement;
- interact/inspect;
- tile inventory;
- meld resolver;
- environmental rewrite;
- memory reconstruction;
- discard decisions;
- echo/ghost replay in later milestones;
- systemic enemy patterns;
- save/load.

## Vertical-slice scope

Target 20–30 minutes.

Locations:

1. Rain Alley
2. Tea House
3. The Falling Table
4. East Arcade
5. Memory Room
6. Dealer's Court

Mechanics introduced:

1. inspect;
2. collect a tile;
3. complete a sequence;
4. form a pair;
5. discard a memory;
6. use an enemy pattern against itself.

Boss:
**The Dealer** — an encounter in which the floor layout is redrawn by Winds and the player must read the discard row to predict the next transformation.

## Player verbs

- Move
- Look
- Interact
- Inspect
- Pick up tile
- Place tile
- Rotate/choose slot
- Call a valid relationship
- Discard
- Reconstruct
- Refuse a false premise

## Anti-features for first release

Do not add:

- open world;
- crafting;
- loot rarity;
- RPG levels;
- skill trees;
- procedural city generation;
- multiplayer;
- live service;
- complex melee combat;
- backend accounts;
- generative AI dialogue in runtime.

These features dilute the core.

## Art direction

Original stylized 3D:

- jade, ink-black, ivory, brass, wet stone;
- warm tea-house light against cold streets;
- oversized furniture;
- impossible corridors;
- floating tile motifs;
- paper screens and geometric shadow patterns.

Do not imitate Disney's Alice appearance. Alice should have an original silhouette and costume.

## Audio direction

- sparse room tone;
- rain;
- porcelain clicks;
- tile shuffles;
- distant bells;
- positional whispers;
- music only for structural transitions or encounters.

## Accessibility baseline

- remappable or at least documented controls;
- keyboard navigation for menus;
- subtitles;
- master/music/SFX volume;
- reduced camera shake option;
- motion sensitivity option for strong spatial warping;
- high-contrast interact indicator.
