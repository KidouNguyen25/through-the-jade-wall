# NARRATIVE BIBLE

## Premise

Alice enters Jade Court after following a blank white tile through a rain-soaked alley. Every inhabitant calls her by a different name. The city says she arrived late to a game that began decades ago.

The city is built around **The Wall**, an impossible structure made from 144 archetypal memory-tiles. Whenever a complete interpretation of the past is formed, Jade Court attempts to end. It never succeeds because one fragment remains contradictory.

Alice is that contradiction.

## Narrative question

**If a coherent story can be assembled from incomplete memories, does coherence make it true?**

Secondary questions:

- Is forgetting a form of violence or mercy?
- Is a person still the same person after their memories are rearranged?
- Does responsibility require certainty?
- When rules are unjust, is mastering them enough?

## Principal characters

### Alice

Not necessarily her real name.
She is observant, persistent, and initially assumes the city has a correct answer hidden somewhere.
Player choices gradually define whether she values certainty, compassion, or autonomy.

### The White Tile

A blank tile that appears in impossible places.
It never speaks.
It is both guide and missing evidence.

### The Dealer

A masked host who treats causality as etiquette.
He believes every unresolved event is simply an unfinished hand.

### East — The Magistrate

Represents institutional order.
Claim: the city survived only because someone made an unforgivable decision.

### South — The Physician

Represents preservation.
Claim: memory is unreliable because trauma protects itself.

### West — The Captain

Represents action.
Claim: hesitation, not violence, destroyed the city.

### North — The Child

Appears in memories that predate and postdate their documented death.
This contradiction destabilizes every official account.

### The Red Queen analogue: The Vermilion Regent

An original character, not a direct adaptation.
She does not demand executions. She demands **validity**:

> “If it cannot be proven, it cannot be permitted.”

She is the embodied authority of the memory engine.

## Story structure

### Act I — Down the Tile

- Rain Alley.
- Blank tile leads Alice into Tea House.
- Three invisible players are finishing a hand.
- Table collapses into impossible depth.
- Alice lands in Jade Court.
- First rule: complete `2 Bamboo + 3 Bamboo + 4 Bamboo` to make three disconnected balconies align.

Player belief: "I am trapped in a strange city."

### Act II — Everyone Remembers You Differently

- East Arcade.
- NPCs identify Alice as messenger, witness, suspect, and child.
- Player learns pair mechanic.
- Two compatible memories create a temporary identity.
- Alice accesses a reconstruction of "The Night of Falling Lanterns."

Player belief: "I was here before."

### Act III — The Discard

Player must choose one of several memory fragments to discard to cross a gate.
The discarded memory stops appearing in NPC dialogue and environmental reconstructions.

Player belief: "My actions do not change the past, but they change what the system can know."

### Act IV — Dealer's Court

The Dealer reveals:

- Jade Court is not preserving history;
- it is repeatedly attempting to produce a legally valid account;
- each valid account condemns someone different.

Player belief: "The system is optimizing for closure, not truth."

### Act V — Vertical-slice conclusion

The boss puzzle offers a forced choice between two suspects.
A hidden third solution is to invalidate the premise by breaking the pair that defines the courtroom's identity.

End line:

> “A hand may be complete and still be wrong.”

The White Tile gains a single mark.

## Full-game long-term reveal

Alice is not one historical person. She is a reconstruction assembled from fragments that the system could not assign without contradiction.

The blank white tile is not "empty." It is the right to remain unclassified.

## Narrative implementation rules

- Exposition must be delivered through spatial evidence, interaction, reconstruction, and short dialogue.
- No lore dump longer than necessary for player comprehension.
- Every major reveal must retroactively explain at least one earlier oddity.
- Contradictions must be deliberate and tracked in data; accidental continuity errors are unacceptable.
- Player choices change available interpretations, not a visible morality score.
- Dialogue choices should represent reasoning or priorities, not cosmetic tone variants.

## Content provenance

Use public-domain literary motifs from Lewis Carroll only at the level of broad motifs, logic, paradox, scale, dream structure, and public-domain wording when legally appropriate. Do not use protected visual designs, music, or modern adaptation-specific characters/assets.
